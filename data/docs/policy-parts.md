Which parts we put on bikes, and when the counter can quote one.

## Like-for-like replacements

The counter quotes replacements that match what's on the bike: the same standard, size and shifting system. Our parts lookup checks every part against the bike's specs and only lists ones that fit. Never quote a part the lookup marks incompatible.

## Upgrades are a mechanic's call

Changing standards or sizes is an upgrade, not a replacement: a bigger rotor, a different freehub body, a wider-range cassette, a different brand's drivetrain. Upgrades often need extra parts (adapters, freehub bodies, sometimes shifters) and a mechanic's judgment. The counter can note the customer's interest; the mechanic decides and quotes it.

## Unverified parts

If the parts lookup can't confirm a fit (marked unknown, for example because we don't have the bike's caliper or tire clearance on file), the part can go on a draft only marked "unverified, mechanic to confirm". Never tell the customer an unverified part will fit.

## Chains

We fit SRAM chains of the right speed by default; they work across nearly every drivetrain. We keep Shimano and other chains for customers who ask. Two exceptions need their own chain: SRAM Transmission (T-Type) bikes need a T-Type chain, and SRAM Flattop drivetrains (for example the newer 12-speed Apex) need a Flattop chain.

## Pricing

Labor is flat-rate from the service menu, not hourly. Jobs priced per wheel, per brake or per cable are charged once per unit. Parts are charged at the catalog price.
