Many suspension complaints are settings, not broken parts. Check the setup before quoting a service: a setup is `SUSPENSION-SETUP`, a lower leg service is `FORK-LOWER-SERVICE`.

## Setup first: sag, pressure, rebound

Before blaming the fork or shock, check the setup for the rider's weight: air pressure (or spring), sag, rebound and compression settings. A lot of "broken suspension" is a bike set up for someone else, or never set up at all. Setting it up is `SUSPENSION-SETUP`, an adjustment.

## Packing up

Suspension that gets firmer and sits lower over a series of quick hits is "packing up". The usual cause is rebound set too slow: the fork or shock can't return before the next hit. That's an adjustment (`SUSPENSION-SETUP`). If it persists with rebound opened up, the damper may need service (`FORK-FULL-SERVICE` or `SHOCK-FULL-SERVICE`).

## Bouncing (pogo)

Suspension that springs back too fast and bucks the rider usually has rebound set too fast. That's an adjustment. If turning the rebound dial makes no difference, the damper has likely lost oil or failed, which is a service.

## Bottoming out

Suspension that uses all its travel with a harsh clunk on ordinary hits usually has too little air pressure for the rider, or needs a volume spacer. That's setup. Bottoming with correct pressure can mean an internal air leak; that's a service.

## Harsh or not using its travel

Suspension that feels harsh and never uses its full travel usually has too much pressure, compression set too firm, or the lockout left on. If the setup is right and it still feels harsh, it's usually overdue for a lower leg service (`FORK-LOWER-SERVICE`), with dry seals and old oil.

## Sticky or slow to move

Suspension that sticks at the top of its travel, or moves notchily, usually needs a service: dry seals, old oil, or worn bushings. Check the lockout isn't half on first. For forks, a lower leg service often fixes it; a rear shock may need an air can service (`SHOCK-AIRCAN-SERVICE`).

## Leaking oil or losing air

Oil on the stanchions or a fork that loses air means failed seals: a service (`FORK-LOWER-SERVICE` or `FORK-FULL-SERVICE`), or `SHOCK-AIRCAN-SERVICE` for a shock losing air. If oil has reached the brake rotor or pads, it's safety-critical: the brake is contaminated and the bike shouldn't be ridden until the brake is fixed too.

## Knocking from the front

A knock or clunk from the front of the bike is often blamed on the fork, but a loose headset feels and sounds almost the same. Check headset play first by holding the front brake and rocking the bike; slight headset play is needs-service and a quick fix (`HEADSET-ADJUST`), while a headset loose enough that the fork visibly moves is safety-critical. A genuine fork knock usually means worn bushings, a service. A knock from the rear can be worn frame pivots (`PIVOT-SERVICE`) or shock bushings.
