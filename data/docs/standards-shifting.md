title: Shifting systems and derailleurs
doc_type: standards

Derailleurs and shifters only work together within the same shifting system.

## Cable pull: why brands and families don't mix

A shifter pulls a set amount of cable per click, and the derailleur is designed to move a set distance for that pull. Different systems use different ratios, so a derailleur from one system won't line up with another's shifter even at the same speed count. It isn't just Shimano vs SRAM: within Shimano, road and mountain bike shifting differ at 10, 11 and 12 speeds.

## Shimano families

Shimano 9-speed road and mountain parts share cable pull. At 10 speed, mountain bike (Dyna-Sys) and road differ, and Tiagra 4700 and GRX 10-speed use a different road ratio from older 10-speed road parts like 105 5700. At 11 and 12 speed, road and mountain bike are separate again. Di2 is electronic and only works with Di2 parts. CUES (LinkGlide) is its own family.

## SRAM families

SRAM Eagle 12-speed mechanical mountain bike parts work together. Newer mechanical 12-speed road and gravel (XPLR) is its own family. AXS is wireless. Eagle Transmission (T-Type) is wireless and mounts directly to the frame.

## T-Type and UDH

SRAM Transmission (T-Type) derailleurs mount straight to the frame instead of on a hanger, and only fit frames built for the Universal Derailleur Hanger (UDH). A non-UDH frame can't take a T-Type derailleur. T-Type drivetrains also need T-Type cassettes and chains.

## Derailleur hangers

Most derailleurs bolt to a replaceable hanger. A bent hanger is one of the most common causes of poor shifting after a crash or a tip-over. Hangers are frame-specific and frames sometimes change mid-year, so match a replacement by photo. Straightening a hanger is `HANGER-ALIGN`.
