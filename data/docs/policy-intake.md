How the counter takes a bike in, and what we charge just to look.

## Identify the bike first

Pick the bike from the catalog whenever you can: make, model and year. The same model name can have completely different parts in different years; a 2012 Fuel EX 8 and a 2023 Fuel EX 8 Gen 6 share almost nothing. If the customer doesn't know the year, ask for a photo or check the serial and frame stickers before quoting parts. For derailleur hangers, match by photo, not model year, because frames change mid-year.

## One row per complaint

Log each thing the customer mentions as its own row, with what they describe in their words in the note. "Creaks only when standing" or "started after a crash" is often the most useful thing on the form. If the customer's story doesn't match the picklist, the note wins.

## Inspection fee

A look-over is `INSPECT`, $25. It's waived if the customer books the work we find. A pre-ride safety check is `SAFETY-CHECK`.

## Cosmetic complaints

When the problem is only cosmetic (a scratch, a scuff, faded paint), we look it over, confirm nothing functional or structural is wrong, and hand the bike back. Never call damage on carbon cosmetic at the counter; that's a mechanic's call. See the safety policy.

## When nothing is wrong

Sometimes the bike is doing exactly what it should. Explain why it's normal, in plain terms, and don't sell a fix for something that isn't broken. Recommending an adjustment or part "just in case" costs the customer money and costs us their trust.

## Estimates are drafts

Every estimate from the counter is a draft. A mechanic reviews it, can change it, and approves it before it becomes a work order. Tell the customer the price may change once a mechanic has looked at the bike.
