---
title: Diagnosing creaks, clicks and squeaks
doc_type: diagnosis
complaints: [creak, click, squeak]
---

Noises travel through a bike, so the part that seems to make the noise often isn't the cause. When the noise happens is the best clue: standing or seated, pedaling or coasting, every pedal stroke or every wheel turn.

## Creak only when seated

A creak that happens when the rider sits and goes away when they stand comes from the seated contact points: the seatpost in the frame, the seat clamp, or the saddle rails in the post head. It's rarely the bottom bracket, even if the customer thinks so. On a rigid post the fix is usually a clean, grease and re-torque (`SEATPOST-ADJUST`), an adjustment. On a bike with a dropper post, a seated creak can come from the dropper itself, which is a service (`DROPPER-SERVICE`).

## Creak when pedaling hard or standing

A creak under pedaling load, especially standing, points at the drivetrain's load path: pedals, cleats, crank arms, chainring bolts and the bottom bracket. Check the cheap things first. Pedal threads and cleats creak often and are easy to fix. Press-fit bottom brackets (PF92, BB86, BB30 and similar) are known creakers; a creaky press-fit BB usually needs to be pressed out and replaced (`BB-INSTALL-PRESSFIT`). Threaded bottom brackets creak less often (`BB-INSTALL-THREADED`).

## Creak from the front of the bike

A creak when pulling on the bar or braking hard can come from the headset, the stem or bar clamps, or a loose front thru-axle. Check for play by holding the front brake and rocking the bike. Slight play is needs-service, usually an adjustment (`HEADSET-ADJUST`); worn bearings need a service (`HEADSET-SERVICE`). Bars or a fork that visibly move are safety-critical.

## A creak that could be a cracked frame

A new creak after a crash, or one that doesn't go away when the usual suspects are fixed, can be a cracked frame, fork or bar. Look for paint cracks near welds, the head tube, the bottom bracket shell and the dropouts. Treat a possible crack as safety-critical: the customer shouldn't ride the bike until a mechanic has inspected it. On carbon, a "scratch" after a crash is never cosmetic at the counter.

## Clicking every pedal stroke

A click once per pedal revolution usually comes from pedals, cleats, a crank arm or chainring bolts, or the bottom bracket. A click on both sides points at the bottom bracket or the crank interface; on one side, that side's pedal, cleat or crank arm. A loose thru-axle or quick release can also click under load.

## Clicking every wheel revolution

A click once per wheel turn comes from the wheel: a loose spoke, something stuck in the tire, a magnet, or a loose axle. Check spoke tension and the tire before anything else.

## Squeaks

A squeak while pedaling is very often a dry or dirty chain; a clean and lube or `DRIVETRAIN-CLEAN` fixes it. Full-suspension bikes can squeak from dry or worn frame pivots (`PIVOT-SERVICE`). A squeak from the saddle area is usually the saddle rails. A squeal when braking is a brake issue, not a drivetrain one; see brake noise.
