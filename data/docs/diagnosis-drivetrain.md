---
title: Diagnosing shifting problems, skipping and dropped chains
doc_type: diagnosis
complaints: [poor_shifting, skipping, chain_drop]
---

Most drivetrain complaints come down to four things: cable tension, a bent hanger, wear, or the wrong parts mixed together.

## Poor shifting after a new bike or new cables

New cables stretch and housing settles in the first few rides, so shifting drifts out of adjustment. Slow shifts in one direction or a gear that won't settle usually just need indexing (`DRIVETRAIN-ADJUST`), an adjustment. Old, gritty or kinked cables and housing need replacing (`SHIFT-CABLE`).

## Poor shifting after a crash or tip-over

If shifting went bad after the bike fell on its drive side, suspect a bent derailleur hanger. A bent hanger makes the derailleur sit crooked, so no amount of barrel-adjuster fiddling fixes it. Straightening it is `HANGER-ALIGN`; a badly bent or cracked hanger needs replacing, matched by photo. Also check the derailleur itself and its cage for damage. A derailleur that's been pushed into the spokes is a safety issue until checked.

## Skipping under load

A chain that skips or jumps when the rider pushes hard, especially on the cogs they use most, usually means a worn chain and cassette. If the chain was recently replaced and it now skips, the cassette wore along with the old chain; the fix is a new cassette (`CASSETTE-INSTALL`), not an adjustment. Skipping can also come from a stiff chain link, poor indexing or a bent hanger.

## Ghost shifting

Gears that change on their own over bumps usually come from cable housing that's too short or poorly routed, a loose shifter clamp, or a frame that flexes the housing. On full-suspension bikes, housing pulled by suspension movement can cause it. It's usually an adjustment or a cable job, not new parts.

## Chain drops

On a 1x drivetrain, chain drops usually mean a worn narrow-wide chainring, a derailleur clutch that's switched off or worn, or a chain that's too long. On a 2x drivetrain, the front derailleur's limits or position are the usual cause. A chain dropping into the spokes or between the cassette and frame needs the limit screws checked before the bike is ridden.

## Mixing parts that don't belong together

A drivetrain that never shifted right after a parts swap may have mismatched parts: a derailleur and shifter from different cable-pull families, a cassette from a different system, or the wrong chain. Check the parts against each other before adjusting anything; no adjustment fixes an incompatible combination.
