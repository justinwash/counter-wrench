---
title: Bottom brackets and crank spindles
doc_type: standards
---

A bottom bracket has to fit two things: the frame's shell and the crank's spindle.

## Threaded shells: BSA, T47, Italian

Threaded bottom brackets screw into the frame. BSA (English) is the most common: 68 mm shells on road bikes, 73 mm on most mountain bikes. T47 is a larger threaded shell used on many newer Trek and gravel bikes; some T47 bottom brackets put the bearings inside the shell. Italian threading is mostly on older Italian road frames. Threaded bottom brackets rarely creak and are easy to service.

## Press-fit shells: BB86, PF92, PF30, BB30, BB386EVO

Press-fit bottom brackets press into the frame with no threads. BB86 (road) and PF92 or BB92 (mountain) take Shimano-style press-fit cups. BB30 presses bearings directly into the frame for 30 mm spindles; PF30 uses cups in a larger shell. Press-fit bottom brackets are known for creaking when the shell or cups wear, and a creaky press-fit BB is a very common shop job.

## Crank spindles

The crank's spindle sets the bearing size. Shimano Hollowtech II uses 24 mm. SRAM DUB is 28.99 mm. Older SRAM GXP is 24 mm on one side, 22 mm on the other. BB30-style cranks are 30 mm. Entry-level and older bikes use square taper or splined (like SRAM Powerspline) spindles. A bottom bracket has to match both the shell and the spindle: a DUB crank won't run in a 24 mm Shimano BB.

## Adapters and conversions

Adapter bottom brackets exist (for example running a 24 mm crank in a BB30 frame), but whether one suits a given frame is a mechanic's call. The counter quotes the bottom bracket that matches the bike's shell and crank as fitted.
