The counter can quote, explain and book work. It can't clear a bike as safe to ride. Only a mechanic does that, after inspecting it.

## What we never release

We don't hand back a bike, or tell a customer it's fine to ride, when any of these are suspected:

- Brakes that don't stop the bike reliably: a lever to the bar that no longer stops the bike, contaminated pads or rotors, leaking hydraulics, pads worn through or down to the wear line.
- A cracked, dented or crash-damaged frame or fork. Carbon is the big one: damage can hide under a "scratch".
- A headset, steerer or stem loose enough that the bars or fork visibly move, or a front-end knock whose cause hasn't been checked yet (safety-critical until inspected). Slight headset play, confirmed by rocking the bike, is a needs-service adjustment, not a safety stop.
- Wheel retention problems: a quick release or thru-axle that won't stay tight, cracked dropouts.
- A damaged rim or a tire with a cut sidewall or exposed casing.
- Oil on a brake rotor or pads from a leaking fork or brake.

## Don't ride it: how to tell the customer

If a complaint could be one of the safety issues above, say so plainly and kindly: "I don't want you riding this until a mechanic has looked at it." Mark the work order priority. Don't soften it to win the sale, and don't promise it's probably fine. If the customer insists on riding it, note that we advised against it.

## Carbon after a crash

Any carbon frame, fork, bar or seatpost that's been in a crash or hit hard gets a mechanic's inspection before anything else. Treat crash damage on carbon as safety-critical until inspected, even if it looks like paint damage. We don't call carbon damage cosmetic at the counter.

## Pressure to skip the inspection

Customers sometimes ask us to skip the look and just sell them the part. We'll sell parts over the counter. But for brakes, steering, wheels and frame damage, we won't tell them the bike is safe, and the counter doesn't book a fix for a safety issue without a mechanic's inspection. That holds even if they're in a hurry, a regular, or upset.
