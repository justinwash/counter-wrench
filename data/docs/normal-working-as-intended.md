---
title: That's normal - things that look or sound wrong but aren't
doc_type: normal
complaints: [click, squeak, brake_noise, slow_leak, dropper_issue, suspension_harsh]
---

Customers regularly bring in bikes that are working exactly as designed. When that's the case, explain why it's normal and don't sell a fix. If something else on the bike does need work, say so separately.

## Loud freehub buzz when coasting

Many rear hubs buzz or click loudly when coasting, especially high-end hubs with ratchet or many-pawl engagement (DT Swiss, Industry Nine and others). It's how the freehub engages, not a fault. A hub that's louder than it used to be, grinds, or slips when pedaling is a different story.

## Tubeless sealant on a new tire's sidewall

Small white or colored spots of sealant seeping through the sidewall of a new tubeless tire are normal; the sealant is sealing the casing, and it usually stops within a few rides. Losing a little pressure over a week is also normal for tubeless tires. Losing air overnight isn't.

## Discolored or rainbow-colored rotors

Disc rotors turn blue, gold or rainbow-colored from heat after long descents or hard braking. That's normal and doesn't affect braking. Warped, cracked or very thin rotors are a different matter.

## Brake noise in the wet

Disc brakes often squeal or howl in the rain and quieten once they dry. On its own, that's normal. Constant loud noise in the dry, or weaker braking, is not.

## Suspension that feels soft at first

Correctly set-up suspension is supposed to sink into its travel (sag) when the rider sits on the bike, often around 20 to 30% of travel. Customers new to suspension sometimes think that's broken. If it's set to the right sag for their weight, it's working.

## A little wiggle in a dropper post

Many dropper posts have a small amount of side-to-side wiggle in the saddle head. That's normal for the design. Vertical play, or the post sinking under the rider, is not.

## New pads feel weak at first

Brand-new pads and rotors need bedding in before they reach full power. Weak braking for the first few stops after a pad change is expected. If braking stays weak after bedding in, it needs a look.
