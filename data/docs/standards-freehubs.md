---
title: Freehub bodies and cassettes
doc_type: standards
---

The freehub body is the splined part of the rear hub that the cassette slides onto. A cassette only fits the freehub it was made for.

## HG (HyperGlide)

HG is Shimano's long-running spline, shared by SRAM's PG cassettes. It takes 8 to 11-speed mountain cassettes, Shimano 11 and 12-speed road cassettes, and SRAM's 12-speed PG Eagle cassettes (like PG-1210 and PG-1230). Its smallest cog is 11 teeth.
An 8 to 10-speed cassette on an 11-speed road HG body needs a 1.85 mm spacer behind it.

## Micro Spline

Micro Spline is Shimano's 12-speed mountain freehub, needed for 10-tooth smallest cogs (for example 10-51 cassettes). Shimano 12-speed mountain cassettes only fit Micro Spline. An HG cassette doesn't fit a Micro Spline body, and a Micro Spline cassette doesn't fit HG.

## XD and XDR

XD is SRAM's driver for cassettes with a 10-tooth cog, used by Eagle XD cassettes and Transmission (T-Type). XDR is the road version, 1.85 mm longer. An XD cassette fits an XDR driver with a 1.85 mm spacer; an XDR cassette doesn't fit XD.

## Changing freehub standards

Going from one freehub standard to another (say HG to Micro Spline to run a Shimano 12-speed cassette) means swapping the freehub body if the hub has one available, or replacing the wheel. It often means derailleur and shifter changes too. That's an upgrade for a mechanic to quote, not a cassette swap.

## Cassettes match the shifting system too

Fitting the freehub isn't enough. Cog spacing and range belong to a shifting system: a Shimano road 12-speed cassette and a SRAM Eagle 12-speed cassette both fit HG, but they don't shift on each other's drivetrains. Match the cassette to the bike's drivetrain family as well as its freehub.
