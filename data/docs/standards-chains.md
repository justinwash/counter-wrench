What makes a chain fit, and when to replace it.

## Match the speed count

A chain has to match the drivetrain's speed count: 12-speed chains are narrower than 11, and so on down. A SRAM chain of the right speed works on nearly any drivetrain, which is why it's our default. Shimano and other chains are available for customers who ask.

## Drivetrains that need a specific chain

Two exceptions need their own chain type. SRAM Eagle Transmission (T-Type) needs a T-Type chain. SRAM Flattop drivetrains, including the newer 12-speed Apex and other SRAM 12-speed road and gravel groups, need a Flattop chain. A regular 12-speed chain isn't compatible with them.

## Chain wear and when to replace

Chains stretch as they wear. We replace them at about 0.5% wear on 11 and 12-speed drivetrains and 0.75% on 10-speed and below. Replaced early, a chain protects the cassette and chainrings; left too long, it wears them too.

## New chain skips on an old cassette

If a new chain skips under load on the cogs the rider uses most, the cassette (and sometimes the chainrings) wore along with the old chain. The fix is a new cassette, not adjusting the derailleur. This is why we check chain wear at every tune-up.
