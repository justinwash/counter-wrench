---
title: Diagnosing brake rub, noise, soft levers and weak braking

Brakes are a safety system. When in doubt, the bike stays with us until a mechanic has looked at it.

## Disc brake rub

A rotor that rubs the pads usually means the caliper isn't centered, the rotor is slightly bent, or the wheel isn't seated straight. Check the thru-axle or quick release is tight first: a wheel that's slightly crooked in the dropouts causes rub. Re-centering the caliper is `BRAKE-ADJUST`; a bent rotor can often be trued (`ROTOR-TRUE`). Rub that comes and goes with each wheel revolution points at the rotor; constant rub points at the caliper.

## Rim brake rub

Rim brake pads rub when the wheel is out of true, the caliper is off-center, or the wheel isn't seated straight. Truing the wheel is `WHEEL-TRUE`; centering the brake is `BRAKE-ADJUST`. A rim with a crack, a bulge or a worn-through braking surface is a safety issue.

## Squealing or howling brakes

Disc brakes that squeal, especially when wet, are often fine, but loud, constant howling usually means contamination or glazed pads. Contaminated pads (oil, chain lube, cleaning spray) also brake weakly, which makes it a safety issue. Contaminated pads get replaced (`BRAKE-PAD-INSTALL`), and the rotor cleaned or replaced.

## Grinding when braking

Metal-on-metal grinding when braking usually means the pads are worn down to their backing plates. That's safety-critical: the bike shouldn't be ridden, and the rotor or rim may already be damaged. Treat this as a brake failure even if the customer only described it as a "squeak" on the form; the note wins over the picklist.

## Soft or spongy lever

On hydraulic brakes, a lever that feels soft, or comes back further each pull, usually means air in the system, which needs a bleed (`BRAKE-BLEED`). A lever that pulls to the bar is safety-critical: the brake may not stop the bike. Very worn pads can also make the lever travel further. On mechanical brakes, a soft lever usually means cable stretch or worn housing (`BRAKE-CABLE`).

## Weak braking

Brakes that stop poorly with a firm lever usually have contaminated or glazed pads, or worn rotors. New pads need bedding in before they reach full power. Leaking hydraulic fluid, oil on the rotor from a leaking fork, or pads that have soaked up lube are safety issues: the customer shouldn't ride until it's fixed. On rim brakes, worn pads or a dirty or wet rim are the usual causes.
