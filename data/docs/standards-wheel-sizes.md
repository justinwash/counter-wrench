---
title: Wheel and tire sizes
doc_type: standards
---

Wheel sizes go by several names. What matters is the bead-seat diameter (BSD), the ISO number in millimeters.

## 700c and 29" are the same size

700c and 29" wheels are the same rim size: 622 mm bead-seat diameter. A "700c" tube or tire fits a 29er, and a 29" tire fits a 700c road or gravel rim, if the width suits the rim and frame. The names come from road and mountain bike traditions; the rim is identical.

## 650b and 27.5" are the same size

650b and 27.5" are the same rim size: 584 mm bead-seat diameter. Older 26" mountain bikes are 559 mm, a different, smaller size. A 27.5" tire doesn't fit a 26" rim, and the reverse doesn't either.

## Reading a tire size

Tires carry an ISO (ETRTO) size like 61-622: 61 mm wide on a 622 mm rim. That's the same tire as "29x2.4". Road tires usually say 700x25c, meaning about 25 mm wide on a 622 rim. When in doubt, match the second number (the bead-seat diameter) exactly, then check the width.

## Mixed wheels ("mullet") bikes

Many modern trail and enduro bikes run a 29" front wheel and a 27.5" rear wheel. Front and rear tires and tubes are then different sizes. Always confirm which wheel a tire or tube is for. Some bikes are mullets only in smaller frame sizes, so check the frame size too.

## Wheel size can change with frame size

Some models use smaller wheels on their smallest frames: for example 27.5" on XS and S frames and 29" from M up. Before quoting tires or tubes, confirm the frame size, not just the model.

## Tire width and frame clearance

Every frame and fork has a maximum tire width. Going wider can rub the frame, especially with mud. If we don't have a bike's clearance on file, a mechanic confirms before we sell a wider tire. Very narrow tires on wide mountain bike rims don't seat or support well; don't put road tires on MTB rims.

## Tubes, valves and valve length

Tubes are sized by bead-seat diameter and a width range (for example 700x28-35). The tube's range has to cover the installed tire's width. Valves are Presta (narrow) or Schrader (car-style); match what the rim is drilled for. Deep-section rims need longer Presta valves (60 mm or more).
