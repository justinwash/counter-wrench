---
title: Axles and hub spacing
doc_type: standards
---

A wheel has to match the frame's axle type and spacing.

## Quick release

Older and entry-level bikes use quick-release skewers: 100 mm front, 130 mm rear on road bikes, 135 mm rear on mountain bikes and hybrids. A quick release that won't stay tight, or a lever left open, is a safety issue.

## Thru-axles

Most modern disc bikes use thru-axles, which thread into the frame. Common rear sizes are 12x142 mm (road, gravel, older mountain bikes) and 12x148 mm (Boost, most modern mountain bikes). Super Boost is 12x157 mm. Front is usually 12x100 mm on road and gravel bikes and 15x110 mm (Boost) on mountain bikes.

## Wheels must match the spacing

A 142 mm wheel doesn't fit a 148 mm frame, and a quick-release wheel doesn't fit a thru-axle frame without different end caps or a different hub. Some hubs convert with end caps; that's a mechanic's call.

## A loose axle can feel like other problems

A thru-axle that isn't tight can cause creaks, clicks, brake rub and a vague, wobbly feel. Before blaming the bottom bracket or the brakes, check both axles are tight.
